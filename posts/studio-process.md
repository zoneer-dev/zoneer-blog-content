Most software projects don't fail because of the code. They fail somewhere earlier — a vague scope, a handoff between people who never talk to each other, a proposal that promises everything and commits to nothing. So the process behind Zoneer Studio engagements is built to close those gaps first, before a single screen gets designed.

It's one continuous line, four checkpoints:

**Discovery call.** A conversation about what's actually slowing your team down — not a sales pitch, not a questionnaire. Just a real look at where the spreadsheet breaks, where the workaround lives, where the manual step nobody wants to own actually sits.

**Scoping & proposal.** What gets built, in what order, and for what. Not a vague retainer you can't hold anyone to — a concrete plan you can point back to later.

**Build.** Iterative development with regular check-ins, so delivery day never surprises you with something you haven't seen coming.

**Launch & support.** Shipping isn't the exit line. The part where real usage finds the edges — the input nobody tested, the workflow that only shows up in week three — is still our problem to solve, not yours to discover alone.

The team that scopes your project is the same one that ships it. No handoff chain, no telephone game between the people who understood the problem and the people who wrote the code.

We're still early — currently building alongside a handful of first partners, with case studies to follow as those projects ship. If you've got something worth building, a discovery call is the place to start — no proposal, no pressure, just a real look at what you're trying to do.
