Most bootcamp marketing describes a syllabus. Zoneer Academy is easier to describe as a ticket, because that's closer to how it actually runs: a brief, and acceptance criteria — the same way our own engineers would write up any project, not a PDF of weekly topics.

The acceptance criteria looks like this:

- Shipped a real project end-to-end, not a tutorial clone.
- Worked in a small team with real code review.
- Comfortable across the stack you trained on, not just copying snippets.

The mechanism behind that is a real board — Backlog, In Progress, Review, Shipped — and your tickets move left to right as you actually build things: version control basics, then your first REST API, then a working prototype, through code review with a mentor, a refactor based on that feedback, and eventually a deploy to production and a capstone presentation. It's the same board a working engineering team runs on, not a simulation of one.

A few things we're upfront about rather than dress up: we don't publish a cost or a fixed length on this site, because both depend on the cohort and format, and we'd rather walk you through it directly than have you guess from a webpage. And our first cohort hasn't shipped yet — real graduate stories go here once they do, not invented ones in the meantime.

If "I've never shipped a real project and want to change that" sounds like you, applying starts with a short conversation, not a binding agreement.
