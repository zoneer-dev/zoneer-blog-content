Ask a Cambodian business where their customers actually message them, and the answer is rarely "our app." It's Telegram. Orders, booking requests, support questions, payment confirmations — a lot of it already happens in a chat thread, because that's where people already are.

A Telegram Mini App builds on that instead of fighting it. It's a full product that lives inside Telegram itself — no separate app store listing, no install step, no "download our app to order" friction that loses half the people who'd otherwise just buy.

A few shapes this tends to take:

- **In-chat ordering & booking flows** — the whole transaction happens without leaving the conversation.
- **Bot + Mini App combinations** — a bot handles the quick stuff, the Mini App handles anything that needs a real interface.
- **Account linking & payments** — tying a Telegram identity to an existing account, and handling payment inside the same flow.

The appeal isn't novelty. It's distribution — building where the audience already is, instead of spending the first month of a launch convincing people to go somewhere new. For a lot of businesses here, that's the difference between a feature people actually use and one that quietly sits at zero downloads.
